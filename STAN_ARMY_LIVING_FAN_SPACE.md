# STAN ARMY — MILESTONE 1: THE LIVING FAN SPACE

## The creative challenge

Our current Nova Reign Army prototype looks polished, professional and well organised.

**Perhaps too polished.**

It currently feels more like a beautifully designed music-industry website than a place inhabited, decorated and constantly changed by obsessive music fans.

We need to discover what makes an online community feel genuinely alive.

**The artist is the attraction. The fans are the experience. The founder gets the credit.**

## 1. Research original MySpace before designing

Conduct visual and functional research into the original MySpace experience, particularly between **2003 and 2008**.

Prioritise actual archived pages, screenshots and documented functionality rather than modern nostalgia interpretations.

Research sources:

- Internet Archive / Wayback Machine: https://web.archive.org/web/*/myspace.com
- Archived profile collection: https://forum.spacehey.com/topic?id=243227&sort=new
- Recreation of Tom's 2006 profile: https://github.com/wittenbrock/toms-myspace-page
- Contemporary documentation of MySpace features and customisation

Explore:

- Individual profile layouts and personalisation
- Comments, bulletins, blogs and social interactions
- Music players and artist profiles
- Photo galleries and fan-created content
- Top Friends / Top 8 and community relationships
- GIFs, stickers, backgrounds and custom graphics
- What made pages feel personal, imperfect and inhabited
- How users expressed their identities through design

Pay particular attention to the differences between **the platform's interface and the content users created within it**.

Document the strongest findings briefly, with links and screenshots where accessible. Distinguish historical evidence from interpretation. Do not copy original MySpace branding or layouts directly.

## 2. Rethink the Nova Army experience

Use the research to evolve the existing prototype.

The goal is not to recreate MySpace.

**The goal is to rediscover what made the early social internet feel personal, exciting and genuinely social — then reinterpret it for today's music fans.**

Keep the Stan Army platform modern, functional, accessible and trustworthy.

Allow the community itself to introduce personality, visual experimentation and delightful unpredictability.

Not every fan-created element should look like it came from the same design system.

## 3. Correct the hierarchy

The visual hierarchy should be:

**ARTIST → COMMUNITY → FAN CONTENT → FOUNDER CREDIT**

- Keep Nova Reign as the visual hero.
- Remove the oversized founder panel beneath the artist header.
- Move founder recognition towards the bottom.
- Use a warm, understated credit: "This little corner of the internet was made possible by @jess.wav. Give her some love."
- Preserve all fan stewardship and ownership permissions.

## 4. Make the community feel alive

Introduce visible evidence of community participation through fictional demonstration content.

Explore:

- A prominent, varied community feed
- Posts from fans with genuinely different personalities
- Comments, reactions and ongoing conversations
- Recently shared photographs and discoveries
- Fan artwork and handmade graphics
- Distinctive personal touches within posts
- Community activity that gives visitors reasons to return

Avoid uniform, editorial-looking content cards.

Do not fabricate genuine live activity. Clearly distinguish demonstration data from real activity.

## 5. Fan galleries and videos

Introduce engaging, visual-first experiences for:

**Galleries**
- Concert photography
- Fan artwork
- Memorabilia
- Personal collections
- Shared memories

**Fan videos**
- Concert clips
- Edits
- Reactions
- Covers
- Fan-made tributes

Use local demonstration content or safe embeds. No real upload infrastructure is required yet.

Avoid turning the homepage into an endless catalogue of feature panels. Show compelling previews that lead to deeper experiences.

## 6. Personality and customisation

Explore how fans might personalise their spaces and contributions through:

- Stickers and GIFs
- Custom backgrounds
- Decorative graphics
- Fan slogans and inside jokes
- Distinctive profile treatments
- Optional visual embellishments

Experiment with controlled overlapping elements, varied compositions and expressive content.

The result should feel human rather than randomly chaotic.

Maintain safe, scoped styling and preserve clean-view and pause-animation controls.

## 7. Preserve what already works

Keep:

- Existing Stan Army identity and dark visual language
- Nova Reign artist hero
- Fan-led Army ownership
- Membership and community identity
- Army Chat
- From the Army / From the Artist separation
- Music and events
- Existing theme customisation and saved preferences
- FAN. THE FLAMES. concept

Improve the hierarchy and integration rather than removing working functionality.

## 8. Design principles

**Structure without sterility.**

**Personality without chaos.**

**Community before corporate presentation.**

**Expression over uniformity.**

**Participation over passive consumption.**

A visitor should immediately understand who the artist is, what the community is doing and how they can become part of it.

An Army should feel like something its members have made together, not a promotional microsite.

## Scope and deliverables

This is still a visual prototype.

1. Research and document relevant original MySpace references.
2. Identify the most useful historical interaction and personalisation principles.
3. Apply those findings to a revised Nova Army experience.
4. Use fictional demonstration users and content.
5. Preserve existing functionality and customisations.
6. Ensure desktop and mobile layouts work.
7. Run existing tests and report results.
8. Present the revised experience for visual review.

Do not implement production authentication, databases, payments, real uploads or hosting.

Do not expand the project into a full social-network backend.

**Prioritise the quality of the experience over the quantity of features.**

### The ultimate creative test

When someone enters Nova's Army, they should feel like they've stumbled into a little corner of the internet that belongs to people who genuinely love this artist.

Not a website designed for fans.

**A website made alive by fans.**
