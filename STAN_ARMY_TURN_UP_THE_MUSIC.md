# STAN ARMY — MILESTONE 1: TURN UP THE MUSIC

## The creative problem

The latest Nova Army revision is a significant improvement. Fan posts, personal treatments, galleries, chat and customisation are bringing the community to life.

However, the large promotional artist banner at the top still makes the experience feel like an artist's marketing website.

**We don't need to advertise the artist to their own fans.**

An Army should feel like somewhere you enter and immediately participate.

Our new direction:

**The music is why they came. The people are why they stay.**

## 1. Remove the oversized promotional hero

Remove the large "FEEL IT TOGETHER" banner from the top of Nova's Army.

Replace it with a compact, confident artist identity area containing:

- Artist name and image
- Army name or identity
- Stan count
- Join/enlist action
- Essential artist details

Keep Nova visually recognisable and prominent, but don't let her identity consume the first screen.

Retain the fan-made personality, backgrounds and existing theme customisation.

**The artist is the attraction. The fans are the experience.**

## 2. Introduce THE ARMY PLAYER

Create a prominent, playful media player near the top of the Army.

Research the visual and interaction language of classic Winamp, early desktop music players, MySpace music embeds and 1990s/2000s media interfaces.

We're interested in their tactility, personality, skinnability and sense of fun — not a literal reproduction.

The player should feel like something fans would enjoy looking at, customising and using.

Explore:

- A distinctive, slightly outrageous player interface
- Play/pause and appropriate media controls
- Track information
- A playful visualiser or equaliser treatment
- Expressive skins and decorative details
- A compact default footprint
- An expanded media mode

Avoid making it a sterile modern streaming widget.

**It can be gloriously ugly if the fan wants it to be.**

But it should also be capable of looking beautiful, restrained or completely ridiculous depending on the Army's chosen aesthetic.

## 3. One player, two primary experiences

The Army Player should support:

**MUSIC**
- Artist tracks or approved music embeds
- Playlists and featured listening selections
- Clear attribution and links to original sources

**VIDEO**
- Official music videos
- Performances
- Approved fan videos and edits
- A larger viewing surface when selected

The player should transition naturally between music and video without requiring an entirely separate page.

Consider a future community-curated selection, but don't build a complicated recommendation or voting system at this stage.

Use original demonstration media or legitimate embeddable sources. Do not invent licensed music or imply that Stan Army hosts commercial recordings.

## 4. Let fans customise the player

The existing customisation tool is becoming one of the strongest parts of Stan Army.

Extend its design language to the player.

Explore a small selection of distinctive player skins, potentially including:

- Classic desktop player
- Loud, colourful Y2K
- Minimal modern
- A deliberately questionable retro skin

Allow the player to feel like part of the Army's visual identity rather than an unrelated widget.

Preserve readability, accessibility, safe styling boundaries and existing saved theme behaviour.

Do not break previously saved customisations.

## 5. Get straight to the community

Immediately after the compact identity and player, visitors should encounter meaningful participation:

- Leave your mark
- Army Chat
- Fan conversations
- The communal shoebox
- Community contributions

Avoid stacking promotional slogans, introductory panels and multiple navigation bars before visitors reach the content.

Simplify the first-screen hierarchy wherever possible.

A returning fan should feel like they're walking into their own space, not visiting a landing page.

## 6. Preserve the living fan experience

Do not lose the improvements from the previous revision:

- Distinctive fan post treatments
- Working demonstration note composer
- Hearts and replies
- Galleries, artwork and memories
- Video shelf
- Founder thank-you near the bottom
- Artist versus Army publishing separation
- Fan-led ownership and permissions
- Existing chat and listening-room functionality
- Theme editor, clean view and animation controls

Avoid duplicating existing music or video functionality unnecessarily. Integrate or reuse what's already there.

## 7. The one-stan test still applies

Imagine Nova's Army has exactly one member.

They should be able to:

1. Arrive and immediately encounter music or video.
2. Make the space visually their own.
3. Leave a distinctive contribution.
4. Enjoy being there even when nobody else is online.

No fake activity indicators or manufactured engagement.

The page should be rewarding because of what the fan can do, not because we pretend there's a crowd.

## Scope and deliverables

This remains a local visual prototype.

- Research relevant historical player references.
- Revise the Nova Army's top-of-page hierarchy.
- Implement a working demonstration music/video player using appropriate media.
- Explore and integrate player skins with the existing theme system.
- Preserve current functionality and fan-led principles.
- Check desktop and mobile layouts.
- Run automated tests, lint, TypeScript and build.
- Report what is genuinely interactive versus simulated.
- Provide screenshots if supported by the available browser tools.

Do not implement production streaming infrastructure, music licensing, payments, authentication or a new backend.

Do not redesign the entire platform.

**Prioritise the experience over adding features.**

## The creative test

When I enter an Army, I shouldn't feel like I'm being introduced to a website.

**I should feel like I've walked into someone's room, the music is already there, and I'm invited to make myself at home.**
